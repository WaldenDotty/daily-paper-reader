# AAS 26-777 SPACECRAFT RENDEZVOUS TRAJECTORY GENERATION WITH MODULAR CONSTRAINTS VIA DIFFUSION MODEL COMPOSITION 

## Mariko A. Storey-Matsutani *, Richard Linares †

Emerging mission classes such as on-orbit servicing, satellite inspection, and ac-tive debris removal require trajectory design methods that are adaptable to a variety of mission scenarios. We present a diffusion-based trajectory generation approach for rendezvous and proximity operations (RPO) that enables flexible configuration of mission constraints. First, individual energy-based diffusion models are trained to satisfy distinct constraints such as approach cone and sensor line-of-sight from a set of optimized trajectories. Then, at inference time, the learned energy models can be composed with one another, or with an analytically defined energy field, to enforce specific constraint combinations. We validate this framework with the composition of a learned approach cone model and a learned sensor line-of-sight model, as well as a learned approach cone model and synthetic obstacle avoidance model, both of which yield constraint satisfaction rates that are within 1 percent-age point of the single-constraint models or higher. These results indicate that our compositional diffusion framework can provide a modular approach to RPO tra-jectory design and enable reconfiguration for new constraint combinations without requiring model retraining. 

INTRODUCTION 

In recent years, rapidly growing interest in space-based infrastructure has accelerated the need for advanced trajectory design methods. In particular, emerging mission classes such as on-orbit servicing, active debris removal, and in-space assembly have emphasized a focus on rendezvous and proximity operations (RPO) maneuvers, which require spacecraft to operate in close proximity while satisfying dynamical and safety constraints. 1 The ongoing NASA Artemis program and com-mercial space station development efforts further motivate the need for robust autonomous RPO capabilities to expand human presence in space, as tasks like docking and resupply are prime exam-ples of critical RPO maneuvers. 2

The relative motion dynamics for RPO regimes are commonly modeled using linearized formula-tions such as the Clohessy–Wiltshire (CW) or Hill equations. 1, 3 However, practical missions require further optimization methods to yield fuel-optimal trajectories that are typically prioritized in space missions. 4 Despite the benefit of these linearized models, trajectory design for RPO is fundamen-tally challenging because the close coordination of spacecraft introduces tightly coupled constraints that require full, six degree-of-freedom motion planning. The consideration of approach cone, sen-sor line-of-sight (LOS), plume impingement avoidance, and collision avoidance constraints can introduce nonconvex feasible regions in the state-control space. 1

Given these limitations, recent work has explored learning-based alternatives for the generation of spacecraft trajectories. Reinforcement learning, 9 imitation learning, 10 and transformer-based archi-tectures 11–14 have been used to approximate optimal policies or directly generate feasible trajecto-ries, as well as to warm-start traditional solvers for nonconvex optimal spacecraft control. However, many existing approaches exhibit limited generalization across varying constraint configurations. As a result, changes in constraint geometry or combinations typically necessitate retraining, thus reducing flexibility and limiting deployment in adaptive mission contexts. 15, 16 

To this end, existing research has begun to explore the application of diffusion models as an al-ternative approach to generative trajectory design. 15–17 Diffusion probabilistic models are a type of generative model capable of representing complex multimodal distributions through an iterative denoising process. 18, 19 Originally developed for high-dimensional data such as images, 18 diffusion models learn probabilistic distributions over solution spaces and allow conditioning mechanisms that are well-suited for trajectory generation problems. 20 In particular, the formulation of diffu-sion models as energy-based models further supports model composition and posterior conditional sampling, enabling constraint-flexible trajectory generation without model retraining. 21 

Diffusion-based models have recently been applied to several spacecraft trajectory problems, in-cluding powered descent guidance, low-thrust transfers, and ballistic transfer generation. 15, 17, 22, 23 

These works show that diffusion models can learn complex solution manifolds, provide diverse and high-quality initial guesses, and accelerate warm-starting of indirect and direct optimal control solvers. 15, 16, 23 However, most existing applications focus on single-vehicle scenarios with rela-tively structured constraints, and do not directly address RPO-specific constraints like approach cones, collision avoidance, and sensor line of sight. This gap motivates the compositional diffusion framework for RPO trajectory generation devel-oped in this work. Instead of training a single complex model to satisfy all mission constraints simultaneously, we demonstrate the decomposition of the constraint space into modular compo-nents and train individual diffusion models on distinct constraint classes (e.g. approach cone, sen-sor line-of-sight). At inference time, these models are composed to generate trajectories that satisfy multiple constraints concurrently, enabling reconfiguration to new RPO mission constraint profiles while reducing retraining requirements. 

METHODOLOGY 

We propose a two-phase framework for generating warm-start trajectories for rendezvous and proximity operations (RPO) under modular constraint profiles. In the first phase, individual dif-fusion models are trained on constraint-specific expert trajectories that are generated offline using sequential convex programming (SCP) via the SCPToolbox framework. 24 In the second phase, these 2diffusion models are composed and conditioned at inference time to generate trajectories that satisfy flexible constraint combinations. The following sections detail the key elements of this framework, including data generation, diffusion model training, and composition operations.

RESULTS 

This section evaluates the compositional diffusion-based framework for RPO trajectory genera-tion. Our objective is to determine whether energy-based model composition and synthetic energy shaping can enforce combined constraints at inference time without any additional training. In addi-tion to constraint satisfaction, the models are evaluated on their ability to learn or preserve feasible trajectory dynamics and fuel optimality. Unless otherwise noted, all diffusion trajectories are gen-erated with 50 DDIM steps and metrics are computed over 100 generated samples per model with a 50-step discretization per trajectory sample.

One model is trained on the approach cone constraint of Equation 3 and one on the sensor line-of-sight (LOS) constraint of Equation 4.

In addition to constraint satisfaction, we investigate the dynamic feasibility of the generated sam-ples. Since the denoising process of diffusion sampling simultaneously generates the full trajectory output, it is necessary to validate that the generated state and control values are self-consistent. By re-propagating each sequence of diffusion-generated controls through the 6-DOF dynamics and measuring the resulting position error, we obtain a mean position error of 7.19 ± 3.45 m for the approach cone model and 5.88 ± 3.64 m for the LOS model. Although this dynamic consistency can improve with further model training, the discrepancy motivates the use of diffusion-generated trajectories as warm starts to traditional solvers, which can restore dynamic feasibility. While diffu-sion models can produce trajectories that are comparable to the SCP solutions, they offer no formal guarantee of dynamic feasibility or optimality. Instead, the diffusion model output can be used to warm-start a traditional optimal solver like the SCP solver used to generate the training data, such that the SCP solver can recover a dynamically feasible and locally optimal solution.

DISCUSSION 

This framework couples diffusion-based generative modeling with inference-time energy compo-sition to produce constraint-modular RPO trajectories. The presented results support three claims. First, diffusion models can generate constraint-satisfying RPO trajectories that reduce end-to-end solution time when used to warm-start a traditional SCP solver. The diffusion-warm-started solve with position-based inverse dynamics reduces end-to-end wall-clock time by roughly 30% (includ-ing the cost of generating the diffusion sample) at a control-effort penalty of ≤ 3.5% and with constraint satisfaction preserved. Notably, this speed-up stems less from a reduction in SCP itera-tions than from better-conditioned convex subproblems. The constraint-aware initial guess places the SCP solver closer to the feasible manifold and thus lowers the per-iteration cost. Furthermore, the near-identical converged fuel cost confirms that the warm start trades little to no optimality for 14 speed. In short, the diffusion sample supplies a constraint-aware, near-optimal initial guess that can be paired with a traditional solver to certify dynamic feasibility and local fuel optimality. Second, composition can induce combined constraint behavior without retraining and with min-imal constraint penalty. Product composition yields a model that satisfies the approach cone and sensor-LOS constraints at 99 .3% and 98 .6% of constraint-active timesteps respectively, despite each single-constraint model satisfying the competing constraint at only 24 –37% . Relative to each constraint-specific model, the product modestly exceeds the approach cone constraint satisfaction by 3.2 percentage points, while incurring a slight reduction of 1.0 percentage points in the satisfaction of the LOS constraint. Third, learned energy landscapes can be composed with synthetic energies to extend the frame-work beyond pre-trained models. Across 100 samples, the negation-composed model achieves 

100% satisfaction of the obstacle-avoidance constraint while preserving the base approach cone satisfaction at 96 .5% of constraint-active timesteps. Because the obstacle term is analytic, new keep-out constraints can be specified and enforced at sampling time without obstacle-specific train-ing data or retraining, which can be an advantageous capability for mission scenarios that demand quick adaptation to unseen obstacle configurations. However, an introduced synthetic energy can degrade trajectory smoothness depending on the barrier form and composition parameters, motivat-ing well-tuned barriers or use as a warm start. Several limitations qualify these findings. First, the analyses are empirical, drawn from 100 

samples on a single rendezvous scenario, and should be read as observations rather than guarantees. The warm-start approach remains the only component that certifies dynamic feasibility and local optimality. Additionally, the composition weights and energy parameters were manually tuned for this work, and a real deployment would require systematic calibration. In particular, when constraints act on different state subsets or bind over overlapping time windows, naive weighting can let some energy fields dominate and suppress joint satisfaction. Finally, dynamic consistency is enforced only weakly during generation and is not explicitly considered during composition. The product-composed samples exhibit larger re-propagation error than the single-constraint samples, and should therefore be validated through the warm-start optimizer. Future work could include physics-informed training and composition to tighten dynamic fea-sibility and fuel optimality without the warm-start framework. Additionally, the synthetic energy approach can be expanded beyond obstacle avoidance, to constraints like keep-out and corridor constraints. Finally, further study of the composition method could explore principled weight cali-bration or additional composition operators for new constraint types. 

CONCLUSION 

In this paper, we present a diffusion-based RPO trajectory generation approach, in which inde-pendently trained, single-constraint energy-based models are composed at sampling time to enforce unseen constraint combinations without additional training. Three findings support this framework. First, the single-constraint models can reproduce intended constraint behavior with high fidelity. Second, product composition of the learned models can yield joint satisfaction of nontrivial con-straints. Third, composing a learned energy with a closed-form energy field can extend the frame-work to unseen constraints. Since diffusion samples carry no formal feasibility or optimality guarantee, we pair them with the same sequential convex programming solver used to generate the training data, warm-starting 15 it to cut end-to-end wall-clock time by roughly 30% with minimal control-effort penalty and with constraint satisfaction preserved, yielding a dynamically feasible, locally fuel-optimal maneuver. Inference-time composition thus provides a flexible, retraining-free mechanism for multi-constraint RPO trajectory generation, and the diffusion-warm-started SCP pipeline provides a practical route from constraint-aware samples to deployable trajectories. Broader initial-condition and geometry sweeps, systematic weight calibration, and dynamics-informed training and composition remain as future work.
