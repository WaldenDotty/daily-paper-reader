Abstract —Accurate direction-of-arrival (DOA) estimation is valuable for spatially selective communication in noisy industrial environments. This work investigates a lightweight single-antenna framework in which receiver motion forms a virtual aperture. The receiver uses onboard inertial measurement unit (IMU) headings and two-way-ranging (TWR) measurements to a known fixed beacon, avoiding GPS, optical tracking, and high-precision external tracking of the mobile receiver. The curvilinear virtual-array MUSIC formulation is evaluated numerically using phase-coherent narrowband snapshots over arbitrary trajectories. Hardware experiments validate a range-domain TWR–IMU bearing estimator using corrected and av-eraged ranging observations. In a campaign of 100 consecutive four-revolution sweeps, all trials are retained. Phase-aligned accumulation recovers a 123 mm range-modulation amplitude, in close agreement with the measured 120 mm antenna lever arm. The single-sweep bearing precision is 8.2◦; absolute world-frame accuracy is limited by systematic BNO055 magnetometer drift in the motorized setup. The embedded bearing-estimation computation consumes 144.9 mJ per estimate, approximately 3% of the measured cycle energy in the tested configuration. These results establish the feasibility of onboard range-domain bearing estimation and motivate future experimental validation of phase-coherent MUSIC during free-form mobile trajectories. 

Index Terms —Direction-of-Arrival (DOA), Curvilinear trajec-tory, Radio frequency (RF), Virtual Antenna Array (VAA), Multiple Signal Classification (MUSIC) 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

# THE Radio-Acoustical Virtual Environment (RAVE) frame-work is fundamentally an indoor technology, designed specifically for workers equipped with industrial hearing pro-tection devices (HPDs) in high-noise environments such as underground mines, manufacturing floors, and industrial ware-houses [1]. In these settings, the sky is typically not visible, GPS is unavailable, and the acoustic environment is so loud that workers cannot communicate without electronic hearing protection. RAVE addresses this by providing spatially-aware audio rendering inside the HPD: the wearer receives direction-ally adapted speech from co-workers without removing their hearing protection, preserving both safety and communication. Even where GPS is available, metre-scale horizontal uncer-tainty is not sufficient to define a compact RF virtual array at a 6 GHz carrier. The receiver-side framework avoids GPS and optical tracking of the mobile device while using TWR constraints from a known fixed beacon. Traditional Direction-of-Arrival (DOA) methods commonly rely on physical antenna arrays and must account for effects such as signal coherence and mutual coupling [2]. Represen-tative approaches include BLE-based DOA estimation with phase-noise mitigation [3], nonlinear array interpolation for correlated signals [4], adaptive beamforming [5], fast planar-array estimation [6], single-moving-observer localization [7], phase-difference projection for non-uniform arrays [8], sparse off-grid and optimized coprime-array methods [9], [10], and DFT-based virtual-array extension [11]. While effective in specific scenarios, these methods remain constrained by their respective array geometries, phase ambiguities, grid errors, sensitivity to noise and model mismatch, mutual coupling, or computational cost. Recent advances have demonstrated single-antenna receivers employing linear trajectories and low-cost hardware [12], and dual-antenna receivers in controlled motion [13]. However, these techniques are restricted to linear or predetermined paths. Concurrently, deep learning and data-driven subspace methods [14]–[16] have improved accuracy but remain con-strained by significant computational demands. Coarray-tensor methods [17], hybrid dynamic-subarray architectures [18], and hybrid receiver architectures [19] offer alternative high-resolution designs, though their predefined geometries and hardware configurations limit applicability to the unpredictable movements characteristic of RAVE scenarios. Coprime-array methods additionally require careful ambiguity handling and array-parameter selection [10], [20]. These limitations motivate lightweight, flexible DOA frameworks for resource-constrained wearables and IoT devices. These challenges are particularly critical in RAVE, where users navigate complex and unpredictable paths, and devices must function in real time under stringent hardware and energy arXiv:2608.12029v1 [eess.SP] 12 Aug 2026 2

constraints. Consistent with recent works on consumer in-door localization and direction-finding [21], [22], our research emphasizes a lightweight, embedded solution appropriate for battery-powered consumer electronics. The contribution of this work is primarily system-level rather than algorithmic: (1) We formulate a single-moving-antenna virtual-array MU-SIC model for arbitrary curvilinear trajectories and eval-uate its sensitivity to SNR, snapshot count, source num-ber, multipath, trajectory linearization, position uncertainty, aperture, and element count through numerical simulation. (2) We formulate a receiver-side trajectory framework using onboard IMU headings and TWR ranges to a known fixed anchor, without GPS, optical tracking, or motion-capture measurements of the receiver trajectory. (3) We validate the complete TWR–IMU measurement chain through controlled circular-trajectory experiments. (4) We measure the board-level energy of one complete rang-ing and bearing-estimation cycle on a 170 MHz Cortex-M4 platform and discuss its relevance for GPS-denied RAVE deployments with a known fixed beacon. The rest of this paper is structured as follows. section II introduces the problem formulation. section III outlines the proposed DOA estimation framework. section IV presents in-depth numerical simulations. section V validates the results with experiments. section VI encapsulates the main findings and future directions.

A. System Model 

In this study, we consider the RAVE concept where DOA is needed for audio adaptation with distance and angle. The system consists of a node with a single antenna that moves along a curvilinear trajectory, effectively creating a Virtual Antenna Array (VAA). For validation, we analyse the far-field case, where the beacon periodically sends communication signal packets. The transmitter position rtx is assumed known in the base deployment, corresponding to a pre-surveyed in-frastructure element or a self-reported beacon position. If the transmitter position is unknown, an additional bootstrapping phase using multi-hop TWR is required. To characterize the DOA, as depicted in Figure 1, we define two angles: the first angle, θr, is the azimuth between the direction of the incident wave (from the transmitter) and a fixed reference direction (magnetic north, as provided by the on-board magnetometer). The second angle, θp,m, m = 1, 2, . . . , M, is the orientation of the receiver’s antenna at the m-th position relative to the same

A Monte-Carlo comparison with NMC = 1000 trials per configuration is performed against ESPRIT, MVDR/Capon, DAS, and PDP. All methods are evaluated at f = 6 GHz with 

M = 6, Nsnap = 16, and a true DOA of 30 ◦. In LOS, the proposed curvilinear-VAA MUSIC method achieves RMSE comparable to DAS and PDP over the evalu-ated SNR range. MVDR/Capon approaches similar accuracy at high SNR, whereas ESPRIT has a larger RMSE for the sim-ulated geometry. Under incoherent interference, the proposed method maintains low RMSE at moderate and high SNR, while ESPRIT and MVDR/Capon are more strongly affected. The primary benefit of the proposed approach is its compatibility with a single moving antenna and a reconstructed curvilinear VAA, rather than LOS accuracy alone.

V. E XPERIMENTAL RESULTS WITH UWB R ADIOS 

This section validates the embedded TWR–IMU range-domain bearing estimator on hardware. The phase-coherent MUSIC estimator of subsection III-B is validated in simulation; the hardware campaign evaluates the range-domain estimator (the phase of a rotation-induced range modulation) together with the ranging noise and heading behaviour that govern its accuracy. Three findings structure the results: the SR1020 ranging noise is characterized and averages predictably; the compact rotating aperture produces a range modulation that, although near the noise level on a single sweep, is recovered by phase-aligned accumulation; and each sweep yields a bearing estimate with an inferred precision of 8 .2◦, while the absolute world-frame reference is limited by magnetometer drift in the motorized setup.

B. Ranging-Error Characterization 

The ranging error was measured from static calibration captures of 1000 averaged range readings at fixed anchor–tag distances, so that any observed dispersion reflects measurement noise rather than geometric variation. After Navg = 128 aver-aging, the range-readout standard deviation is σread ≈ 151 mm at 5 m (Figure 13), with a corresponding readout RMSE of 151 mm; at 1.3 m it is ≈ 198 mm. The uncertainty remains of the same order at both distances, suggesting that distance alone is not the dominant dispersion source. Reducing the averaging to Navg = 32 raises σread to ≈ 303 mm, a factor of 2 .01 relative to the N = 128 case, matching the p128 /32 = 2 prediction and confirming that the readout noise averages as 1 /√N.These values characterize the controlled, nominally polarization-aligned setup. During rotation, the 29.8 s capture interval provides approximately 116 reported range observations before validity filtering, corresponding to about 9–10 observations per angular bin on average for M = 12. The actual occupancy varies because of invalid-range rejection and nonuniform angular sampling. The measured per-bin RMSE is ≈ 136 mm because it also includes correlated ranging error, heading misalignment, multipath, and finite-bin variation; thus the static 1 /√N law does not directly predict this residual.

D. Bearing-Estimation Accuracy 

The estimator is evaluated over 100 consecutive automated sweeps at a fixed anchor; all sweeps are retained. The world-frame anchor bearing is referenced through the static IMU heading with the tag antenna aimed at the anchor, read at the start of the campaign ( θref = 104 .0◦). Each sweep produces a complete DOA estimate from the sinusoidal fit over the full rotation. Two effects govern its accuracy. First, the absolute anchor bearing, which should be constant across sweeps, exhibits a systematic drift of 2 .0◦ per sweep (approximately 200 ◦ over the campaign) originating in the BNO055 magnetometer, perturbed by the rotating metallic platform and motor; this drift affects the absolute heading reference, not the range-domain estimator. Second, to quantify the estimator precision independently of this slow drift, we use the standard deviation of the DOA change between consecutive sweeps. A nearly constant drift contributes primarily to the mean of the successive differences rather than to their standard deviation. After removing the mean successive increment, the remaining sweep-to-sweep variation yields a single-sweep DOA precision of σ∆/√2 = 8.2◦, consistent with the near-unity per-bin amplitude SNR and without requiring a parametric drift model. The magnetometer drift, by contrast, is a correlated systematic error that a single heading sensor cannot remove; suppressing it in deployment is precisely the role of the inertial fusion and multi-anchor geometry of the RAVE framework.

This work investigated single-antenna DOA estimation using motion-induced virtual apertures for embedded UWB systems. A phase-coherent curvilinear virtual-array MUSIC formulation was developed and evaluated exclusively through numerical simulations, which quantified the effects of SNR, snapshot count, source number, multipath, trajectory curvature, position uncertainty, aperture, and array size. Experimental validation focused on the embedded TWR– IMU range-domain implementation. Using two SR1020 UWB radios and a controlled circular trajectory with a 120 mm antenna lever arm, the estimator was evaluated over 100 consecutive four-revolution sweeps, all of which were retained. Phase-aligned accumulation recovered a 123 mm modulation amplitude, closely matching the measured lever arm. The inferred single-sweep bearing precision was 8.2◦. Because the BNO055 magnetometer exhibited a systematic drift of approximately 2 .0◦ per sweep in the motorized metallic setup, these experiments characterize estimator precision rather than absolute world-frame accuracy. The embedded computation required 144.9 mJ per estimate, representing approximately 3% of the measured cycle energy. These results demonstrate lightweight onboard TWR–IMU bearing estimation without GPS or optical tracking, assuming a known anchor, while identifying magnetometer stability as the main absolute-heading limitation. Future work will address robust inertial/magnetic and RF-phase calibration, embedded phase-coherent snapshot acquisition and MCU MUSIC imple-mentation, and phase-coherent curvilinear-VAA MUSIC under unconstrained motion. A near-field extension will require a spherical-wavefront steering model together with dedicated numerical and experimental validation. Antenna orientation, polarization mismatch, and body blockage will also be inves-tigated. ACKNOWLEDGEMENTS
