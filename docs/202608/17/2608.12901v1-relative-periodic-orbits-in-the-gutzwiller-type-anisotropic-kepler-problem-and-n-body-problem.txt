# Relative Periodic Orbits in the Gutzwiller-type Anisotropic Kepler Problem and n-body Problem 

## Xijun Hu 1 Yuwei Ou 1 Zhiwen Qiao 1 Yifan Yang 11 School of Mathematics, Shandong University, 27 Shanda Nanlu, 250100 Jinan, P. R. China ∗

Abstract 

We study the relative periodic orbits in the Gutzwiller-type anisotropic Ke-pler problem, which is a generalized model derived from the classical Gutzwiller anisotropic Kepler problem. By reducing the rotational symmetry of the z-axis, we obtain a reduced system with two degrees of freedom and its energy surface forms a compact and regular three-sphere in a certain parameter range. Combin-ing the estimation of the Seifert rotation number of the planar Kepler orbit and the CHHL formula introduced in [6], we prove that this system admits infinitely many periodic orbits on every compact regular energy surface. This model can be applied to the (1 + 2 n)-body problem to find infinitely many relative periodic or-bits with hip-hop symmetry as well as relative periodic orbits in the n-pyramidal problem.

# 1 Introduction and main results 

For the equal-mass 2 n-body problem with n ≥ 2, there exist solutions with hip-hop symmetry. With the center of mass of the 2 n-body system at the origin, such solutions satisfy the following condition: the 2 n bodies are always partitioned into two groups of 

n, each occupying the vertices of a regular n-gon (or the endpoints of a line segment when n = 2). These configurations lie in planes perpendicular to the z-axis, and their orthogonal projections onto the ( x, y )-plane together form a regular 2 n-gon. These solutions were first numerically observed by Davies, Truman, and Williams [8]. Later, Chenciner and Venturelli [5] used a variational approach to obtain collisionless Z/4Z-symmetric action minimizers and coined the term hip-hop to describe orbits whose configuration oscillates between the central configuration of the square and that of the tetrahedron. This framework was subsequently extended to general 2 n-body systems. Specifically, Terracini and Venturelli [28] proved the existence of collisionless, non-planar and non-homographic trajectories with hip-hop symmetry for weak-force potentials r−σ

by variational approach with topological constraints. Using an analytic continuation argument, Barrab´ es, Cors, Pinyol, and Soler [2, 3] proved the existence of families of solutions with hip-hop symmetry that approach planar relative equilibria and highly eccentric elliptic relative equilibria in the Newtonian setting. This class of solutions can be generalized to the (1 + 2 n)-body problem, where a central mass is introduced in addition to the 2 n equal-mass bodies. There exists an invariant subsystem in which the 2 n equal-mass bodies exhibit hip-hop symmetric motion, while the central body remains stationary at the system’s center of mass. Another closely related problem is the n-pyramidal problem, that is, the motion of 1 + n point masses in R3 under Newton’s universal gravitational law. In this system, the latter n identical masses forming a regular n-gon are symmetric about a fixed axis, along which the first particle moves. When n = 2, we obtain the well-known spatial isosceles three-body problem. This problem has attracted extensive research, see [1, 25, 27]. Recently, a symplectic dynam-ical approach for the spatial isosceles three-body problem was developed in [14], where the authors found a Hopf link which spans an open book decomposition whose pages are annulus-like global surfaces of section. They also proved the existence of infinitely many periodic orbits on compact regular energy surfaces when the mass ratio is large 5enough and verified the convexity of energy surfaces. In [13], the authors proved the existence of infinitely many periodic orbits on every compact regular energy surface and found a non-trivial twist interval defined by the rotation number of the Euler orbit and the contact volume of energy surface. It encodes the relative winding of periodic orbits. For non-compact energy surfaces, the authors proved the existence of infinitely many periodic orbits and infinitely many parabolic trajectories. In [18], Hu, Qiao and Yu proved that the n-pyramidal problem possesses infinitely many periodic orbits on com-pact energy surfaces when the mass ratio α small enough. In this paper, we generalize the conclusion to all mass ratios provided that the energy surface remains compact. Following [18], the reduced Hamiltonian of the n-pyramidal problem can be written as

## 2.3 Reeb flows and Rotation numbers 

Let ( M, ξ = ker λ) be a contact three-sphere with contact form λ, which is a 1-form so that λ ∧ dλ becomes a volume form on M . The Reeb vector field R is determined by 

λ(R) = 1 , dλ (R, ·) ≡ 0 and preserves the contact structure ξ := ker λ. ξ is co-oriented 8by λ, on which there is a symplectic form dλ . The flow {ϕt}t∈R of R is called the Reeb flow of ( M, ξ = ker λ). A periodic orbit (or Reeb orbit) of R is a map ζ : [0 , T ] → M ,which is a periodic trajectory of ϕt with period (or Reeb period) T > 0. We call ζ

simple if T is the minimal period of ζ. Denote P the collection of Reeb orbits. Each simple element ζ ∈ P bounds an embedded surface S in M , called Seifert surface, whose dλ -area is equal to T . We call ζ unknotted if S is a disk. Identifying the Reeb orbits, which differ by a time shift. Let τ be a trivialization of ξ over ζ in the distinguished homotopy class determined by S, for which the outward normal vector field to S has zero winding number along ζ. Under the trivialization τ , the transverse flow dϕ t : ξ|ζ(0) → ξ|ζ(t) determines a symplectic path γ(t), t ∈ [0 , T ], in Sp(2). Define the Seifert rotation number of ζ as ˆρ(ζ) := ˆ i(ζ)/2,

where ˆ i(ζ) is the mean index of the sympletic path γ, see Section A. Define the linearized return map of ζ as dϕ T : ξζ(0) → ξζ(T ). A Reeb orbit ζ is a called elliptic if the two eigenvalues of dϕ T lies in the unit circle U ⊂ C. Otherwise, we call ζ a hyperbolic Reeb orbit. A Reeb orbit ζ is called irrational elliptic if ˆ ρ(ζ) is an irrational number. If ( M, ξ = ker λ) is a contact-type three-sphere in ( R4, ω 0) with the standard sym-plectic form ω0 = dy 1 ∧ dx 1 + dy 2 ∧ dx 2 satisfying dλ = ω0, we can choose a dλ -positive frame {X1, X 2} ⊂ T M ,

X1 = n4∂y1 − n3∂y2 + n2∂x1 − n1∂x2 ,X2 = −n2∂y1 + n1∂y2 + n4∂x1 − n3∂x2 .

where n := n1∂y1 + n2∂y2 + n3∂x1 + n4∂x2 is the outward normal vector field on M . In particular, X1, X 2 are transverse to the Reeb vector field R. Then for every ζ ∈ P ,we obtain a trivialization τ0 of ξ over ζ using {X1, X 2} and the projection T M → ξ

along R and it determines a symplectic path γ0(t), t ∈ [0 , T ], in Sp(2).
