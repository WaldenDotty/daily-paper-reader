Abstract 

The quadratic optimization-free (QO-free) method is a class of powerful and effective algorithms for solving nonlinearly constrained optimization problems in Euclidean spaces. The aim of the present work is to extend this method to solve optimization problems on manifolds with additional equality and inequal-ity constraints. We first present a specific algorithm in the manifold setting. At each iteration, three linear systems sharing a common linear operator are solved to determine the master search direction. In addition, a higher-order correction direction is obtained by solving a reduced linear least squares subproblem to circumvent the Maratos effect which is assumed not to arise in existing related literature. A Riemannian arc search is then performed within the tangent space of the current iterate to generate the new iterate. Under appropriate assump-tions, we establish the global and strong convergence of the proposed method. Moreover, we prove that the unit step size will eventually be accepted by the arc search, upon which the superlinear convergence of the algorithm is estab-lished. Finally, numerical results demonstrate that the proposed method is very competitive compared with other existing approaches.

# 1 Introduction 

We consider the following problem min  

> x∈M

f (x)s.t. ci(x) ≤ 0, i ∈ I = {1, · · · , m },ci(x) = 0 , i ∈ E = {m + 1 , · · · , m + ℓ},

(P) where M is a complete Riemannian manifold with dimension n, and f : M → R

and ci : M → R, i ∈ I ∪ E , are twice continuously differentiable functions. Problems of this kind frequently arise in practical applications, such as nonnegative princi-pal component analysis [1], multicontact postures computation of static robot [2], direct trajectory optimization of rigid bodies [3], k-means clustering [4], and robust Wasserstein distance [5]. If M = Rn, then problem (P) reduces to the standard nonlinear constrained opti-mization problem in the Euclidean space Rn. For this case, there are already numerous efficient solution methods available, such as penalty function methods [6], sequential quadratic optimization (SQO) or formerly known as sequential quadratic programming (SQP) methods [7–10], quadratic optimization-free (QO-free) methods [11–20], and interior point methods [21]. On the other hand, if there are no additional constraints in problem (P), i.e., I = ∅ and E = ∅, then (P) reduces to the classical optimization on Riemannian manifolds (or called Riemannian optimization). In this context, a variety of efficient methods have also been developed, such as Riemannian steepest descent method [22], Riemannian conjugate gradient methods [23–26], Riemannian Newton-type methods [27–29], Riemannian trust-region methods [30, 31]. We refer the readers to the books [32–34] and the survey [35] for more details on Riemannian optimization. In contrast to the extensive research achievements gained for the two aforemen-tioned special cases, studies on the general problem (P) remain quite limited. Below, we present a brief review of the relevant literature. In 2014, Yang et al. [36] generalized the linear independent constraint qualification (LICQ), and the optimality conditions including the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions, the second-order necessary con-ditions, and the second-order sufficient conditions (SOSC) to the Riemannian setting. Later, Bergmann and Herzog [37] extended some other constraint qualifications (CQ) from Euclidean spaces to the manifold setting, including Mangasarian-Fromovitz CQ, Abadie CQ and Guignard CQ. In 2018, Brossette et al. [2] proposed a filter-type Riemannian SQO (RSQO) method to solve problem (P) and applied it to the compu-tation of multicontact postures for static robots; however, no convergence analysis was provided. In 2020, Liu and Boumal [38] extended an augmented Lagrangian method and an exact penalty method—originally developed for the Euclidean setting—to the Riemannian setting, and further established their global convergence. Yet, the work 2[38] did not include a local convergence analysis. In 2021, Schiela and Ortiz [39] devel-oped an RSQO method for equality constrained problems (i.e., I = ∅) on Hilbert manifolds, and established its local quadratic convergence. In 2022, Obara et al. [40] proposed a penalty-based RSQO method to solve problem (P), which reduces to the classical Euclidean Han’s SQO method [41] when M = Rn. Note that this is the first algorithm that enjoys both global and local convergence for (P). We also note that while the method in [40] exhibits excellent numerical performance on two classes of test problems, it suffers from two theoretical limitations in general: 1) the quadratic optimization (QO) subproblem solved at each iteration may become infeasible (i.e., inconsistent) when the iteration point violates the equality and/or inequality con-straints, thereby causing the algorithm to fail; 2) the Maratos effect [42] may occur, meaning that close to a solution, the merit function may fail to accept the unit step. In fact, overcoming the Maratos effect is a necessary prerequisite for proving the local fast convergence of the algorithm, yet the method proposed in [40] merely takes this property as an underlying assumption. Recently, Obara et al. [43] applied the method proposed in [40] to the identification of stable linear systems. In 2024, Lai and Yoshise [44] generalized the classical primal-dual interior-point method to the Riemannian setting, derived its local superlinear and quadratic convergence properties within the framework of the perturbed damped Riemannian Newton method, and further estab-lished the global convergence of the algorithm when combined with a classical line search. However, the Maratos effect may still be unavoidable in this context. Very recently, Obara et al. [45] (and its companion paper [46]) proposed a Riemannian interior point method for problem (P), and established the local superlinear conver-gence and local near-quadratic convergence. Furthermore, when E = ∅, they also demonstrated the global convergence by using a trust region approach. From the above observations, we note that the existing methods for solving problem (P) either lack convergence analysis [2], only achieve global convergence [38], only exhibit local convergence [39], or possess both global and local convergence but fail to overcome the Maratos effect [40, 44]. The goal of this paper is to propose a new method for problem (P) that enjoys the following properties: 

• the main subproblems solved at each iteration are always feasible; 

• both global and local fast convergence can be achieved simultaneously; 

• the Maratos effect can be overcome. Some techniques in this paper originate from the works in [14], [47], [48], and [11]. In [14], a Euclidean QO-free method was proposed. Unlike SQO methods, QO-free meth-ods avoid the need to solve QO subproblems; instead, they only require solving systems of linear equations. Over the past few decades, Euclidean QO-free methods have been well-studied and have evolved into diverse forms; see, e.g., [11–20]. The framework of the proposed Riemannian QO-free method mainly follows that in [14]. Since the Euclidean QO-free method in [14] only addresses problems with inequality constraints, our approach adopts the semi-penalty scheme proposed by Mayne and Polak [47] to convert problem (P) into a sequence of inequality-constrained optimization problems 3of the following form: min

In our algorithm, we employ the scheme proposed in [48] to increase ρ, ensuring that it remains fixed after a finite number of iterations. Moreover, we also establish the relationship between problems (P) and (P ρ); see Proposition 3.1 below. At each iteration, three linear systems sharing a common linear operator are solved to determine the master search direction, which yields significant computa-tional advantages compared to using different linear operators. For instance, one can use LU decomposition to solve the linear systems; consequently, the computational cost is essentially equivalent to that of solving a single linear system. These systems are always feasible and admit unique solutions. The first one is solved to generate a descent direction for Fρ, the second one is used to generate a sufficient descent direc-tion, and the third one is to ensure the feasibility of the next iterate. As a result, a master direction with feasible and descent properties is obtained. In order to further overcome the Maratos effect, a higher-order correction direction is produced by solving a reduced linear least squares subproblem. Note that this subproblem does not need to be solved in early iterations. A Riemannian arc search is then performed within the tangent space of the current iterate to generate the new iterate. Our method belongs to the class of feasible methods for problem (P ρ); that is, it starts from a (strictly) feasible point and generates a sequence of (strictly) feasible iterates for (P ρ). It further qualifies as a feasible descent method once the parameter ρ is fixed. Under appropriate assumptions, we establish the global convergence of the pro-posed method. It is worth mentioning that this global convergence can also be achieved even in the absence of the higher-order correction direction. The strong convergence (i.e., the convergence of the whole iteration sequence) is also achieved. Moreover, we prove that the unit step size will eventually be accepted by the arc search, upon which the superlinear convergence of the algorithm is established. Finally, numerical results demonstrate that the proposed method is competitive compared with other existing approaches. As far as we know, our method is the first extension of QO-free methods to the Riemannian setting. Additionally, this is the first time that the Maratos effect has been overcome in the context of solving problem (P). The organization of this paper is as follows. In Section 2, we review some basic results closely relevant to our method. In Section 3, we present the algorithm and discuss its properties. Thus, we first introduce the definition of the gradient of a function on a Riemannian manifold, which is referred to as the Riemannian gradient. 

Definition 2.1 ([32, Sec. 3.6]) Let f : M → R be a smooth function on M. The gradient of f at x ∈ M , denoted by grad f (x), is defined as the unique element of TxM that satisfies 

〈grad f (x), ξ 〉x = D f (x)[ ξ], ∀ξ ∈ TxM,

where Df (x) is the differential of f at x.

Let ¯∇ denote the Riemannian connection, which is used to characterize the deriva-tive of a vector field on M; see [32, Sec. 5] for further details. Based on this, we introduce the concept of Riemannian Hessian that enables us to establish the SOSC for (P). 

Definition 2.2 ([32, Def. 5.5.1]) Given a smooth function f : M → R, the Riemannian Hessian of f at x ∈ M is a linear mapping Hess f (x) : TxM → TxM defined by 

Hess f (x)[ ξx] = ¯ ∇ξx grad f, ∀ξx ∈ TxM,

where ¯∇ξx grad f denotes the derivative of the vector field grad f along direction ξx.

The following notion of retraction is used to define the update of iterates in Riemannian optimization algorithms. 

Definition 2.3 ([32, Def. 4.1.1]) A smooth mapping R : T M → M is called a retraction on M if it satisfies the properties: (i) Rx(0 x) = x, (ii) DRx(0 x) = id x, where 0x denotes the zero element of TxM, Rx and id x denote the restriction of R to TxM and the identity mapping on TxM, respectively.

## 6.1 Problem setting 

Nonnegative low-rank matrix completion. Recovering a matrix from a small number of observed entries constitutes a low-rank matrix completion problem (see e.g., [56, 57]). Let Mr = {X ∈ Rd×s | rank( X) = r} be a fixed-rank manifold and N

be a subset of {1, · · · , d } × { 1, · · · , s }. The nonnegative low-rank matrix completion problem in [40] is given by min

# 7 Conclusions 

In this paper, we have presented a QO-free method for solving Riemannian optimiza-tion problems with equality and inequality constraints. At each iteration, the master search direction is obtained by solving three linear systems, all of which are always consistent. This is not computationally expensive, as these systems share the same linear operator. In order to avoid the Maratos effect, the correction direction is gen-erated by solving a least squares problem, which can be neglected in early iterations. Global convergence as well as strong and superlinear convergence have been proved under mild assumptions. Promising preliminary numerical results were reported. As future work, the proposed method can be further improved. For instance, we may (i) reduce the scale of the linear systems by introducing the active set strategy (see, e.g., [16]), (ii) extend it to handle infeasible initial points by the idea of the strongly subfeasible direction methods (see, e.g., [59, 60]), and (iii) incorporate a trial line search to check whether tk = 1 is acceptable, which can reduce the number of times the least squares problem is solved and the arc search is executed (see, e.g., [17]). In addition, it is of interest to apply our method to other practical problems, such as multicontact posture computation for static robots [2] and stable linear system identification [43]; however, this lies beyond the scope of this paper.
