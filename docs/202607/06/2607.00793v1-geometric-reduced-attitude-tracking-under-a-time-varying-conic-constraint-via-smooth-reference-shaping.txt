# Geometric Reduced-Attitude Tracking under a Time-Varying Conic Constraint via Smooth Reference-Shaping* 

Pedro Santos 1, Joel Reis 2, Paulo Oliveira 1, and Carlos Silvestre 2,3

Abstract — This letter studies reduced-attitude tracking for a rigid body on the 2-sphere S2 under a time-varying conic constraint. Using a kinematic model on S2, we first propose a ge-ometric tracking law that guarantees almost-global asymptotic and regionally exponential convergence in the unconstrained case, where the angular velocity serves as the control input. We then introduce a smooth reference-shaping mechanism that adjusts the desired direction so that the reference provided to the controller satisfies the time-varying conic constraint while preserving the smoothness required by the tracking law. The re-sulting approach yields smooth continuous feedback and retains the stability guarantees of the unconstrained controller, albeit at the expense of enforcing a soft version of the original constraint. Simulation results illustrate the effectiveness of the method and highlight its suitability for applications where deterministic behavior, smooth control action, and strong stability guarantees are preferred over hard constraint satisfaction. 

I. INTRODUCTION Reduced-attitude control of rigid bodies arises in a wide range of applications, motivated by the need to regulate a body-fixed axis and by the presence of torque underactuation. Representative examples include spacecraft spin-axis stabi-lization [1], thrust-direction control for trajectory tracking of underactuated vehicles [2], [3], and visual servoing [4]. Motivated by real engineering problems, recent years have seen a growing interest in the constrained attitude control problem [6], in which specific regions of the configuration space, either SO(3) or S2, must be avoided [7], [8]. The literature on constrained attitude control is vast and can be broadly classified according to control task, constraint type, and solution strategy for handling constraints. The control task may involve reorientation, stabilization or tracking. Con-straints may be single [9] or multiple [10], and can be static or dynamic [11], of the inclusion or exclusion type [12], and may have different geometric structures [13]. Finally, the most predominant solution strategies include reference-shaping approaches [14]–[16]; barrier-based methods, such as barrier Lyapunov functions [17], potential functions [6], [18], and Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) [9], [19]; and optimization-based methods, including convex optimization [20] and Model Predictive Control (MPC) [21]. Relatively few works address attitude trajectory tracking under time-varying constraints. In particular, solutions for conic constraints in which both the cone axis and the half-angle vary with time remain limited. In [12], two dynamic conic constraints under reduced-attitude tracking are handled via artificial potential and Lyapunov barrier functions, al-though the half-angle limits are kept constant. Other works have focused on the reorientation/planning problem with time-varying constraints. For instance, in [22] the authors rely on a search-based method to determine the attitude maneuver between two points, while in [23] an artificial potential field-based method is proposed for the reorientation problem. Moreover, both single and multiple invariant conic constraints have been studied in [9], [24], [25]. In this work, we consider a single dynamic conic con-straint on S2, where both the cone axis and half-angle are known functions of time. This scenario is relevant to appli-cations including angle-of-attack limiting in aerial vehicles [26], where the relative velocity vector is time-varying and the maximum allowable angle depends on flight conditions, as well as spacecraft solar panel and sensor pointing [18], where pointing constraints vary with orbital dynamics. By leveraging a reduced-attitude kinematic model on S2 and treating two orthogonal components of the angular velocity vector as control inputs, we first devise a geometric reduced-attitude tracking law for the unconstrained case. Related approaches to the unconstrained reduced-attitude tracking problem can be found in [27]–[29]. We then introduce a smooth reference-shaping mechanism that generates an

A. Contributions 

Aiming at strict stability guarantees through smooth feed-back, the main contributions can be summarized as follows:  

> •

We propose a smooth reference-shaping mechanism for a time-varying conic constraint, ensuring admissibility of the shaped-reference and preserving the differentia-bility properties required by the controller;  

> •

We prove that the tracking controller, fed with the shaped-reference, ensures satisfaction of a soft version of the constraint, providing a closed-form solution;  

> •

We quantify the deviation from the optimal projected reference introduced by the smoothing step.

Besides the reference tracking objective, we wish to con-strain the geodesic distance between q and an externally defined, time-parameterized direction a ∈ S2.Definition 1 (Geodesic distance [5]) . The geodesic distance between two points x, y ∈ S2 is defined as the angle between them and is given by dS2 (x, y) := arccos ( xTy), with 0 ≤

dS2 (x, y) ≤ π, which corresponds to the length of the shorter great-circle arc connecting the points. 

The time-varying constraint can be formulated as 

dS2 (q(t), a(t)) ≤ αmax (t) , (5) where αmax ∈ (0 , π ) is an externally defined, possibly time-varying, maximum value for the geodesic distance between q and a. Accordingly, a safe set S is defined as S(t) := q ∈ S2 : dS2 (q(t), a(t)) ≤ αmax (t) . Note that the formulation in (5) can be used to represent both conic inclusion and exclusion constraints. In this letter, we consider a relaxed version of the con-ically constrained attitude control problem, where the hard constraint (5) is replaced by the soft constraint 

dS2 (q(t), a(t)) ≤ αmax (t) + γ(t) , (6) where γ : [0 , ∞) 7 → (0 , π ] is an admissible vanishing violation, satisfying γ(t) → 0 as t → ∞ and ˙γ(t) < 0

for t ≥ 0. The soft-constrained problem can then be stated as follows:

C. Solution Strategy 

Our approach aims to enforce stability guarantees through continuous smooth feedback while avoiding online optimiza-tion. To that end, we start by solving the unconstrained Problem 1 and design a geometric controller that ensures almost global convergence of q to qd. Then, we address Problem 2 by proposing a smooth reference-shaping method such that the condition 

dS2 (qd(t), a(t)) ≤ αmax (t) , (7) where qd ∈ S2 is the shaped-reference supplied to the controller, is always satisfied. The resulting scheme is shown in Fig. 2. As opposed to optimization- and/or CBF-based methods, our strategy foregoes a strict certificate of forward-invariance of the safe set S in the name of improved real-time stability guarantees and smoother control input.

CONCLUSIONS This letter proposed a geometric solution to reduced-attitude tracking on the 2-sphere under a time-varying conic constraint. An unconstrained tracking law was first developed with almost-global asymptotic and regionally exponential stability guarantees. A smooth reference-shaping mechanism was then introduced to generate an admissible reference while preserving the differentiability required by the con-troller. The resulting closed-form scheme provides continu-ous feedback, avoids online optimization, and is therefore attractive for real-time implementation on computationally limited platforms. Although the method enforces a relaxed version of the original constraint rather than hard forward invariance of the admissible set, it offers a useful tradeoff in applications where smooth control action and rigorous tracking guarantees are prioritized.
