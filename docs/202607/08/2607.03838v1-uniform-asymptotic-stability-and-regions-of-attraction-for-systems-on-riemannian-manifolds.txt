# Uniform asymptotic stability and regions of attraction for systems on Riemannian manifolds 

## Li Deng *and Yuan Wang 

School of Mathematics, Southwest Jiaotong University, Chengdu 611756, China 

dengli@swjtu.edu.cn , 3235642106@qq.com 

Abstract 

This paper studies uniform asymptotic stability of equilibrium points of ordinary differential equa-tions on complete Riemannian manifolds and estimates of the corresponding regions of attraction. We first refine the classical Lyapunov stability theorem in Euclidean space and obtain a sharper estimate of the region of attraction. We then extend this result to systems on complete Riemannian manifolds and establish sufficient conditions for uniform asymptotic stability together with an explicit estimate of the corresponding region of attraction. The resulting estimate is constrained by both the injectivity radius at the equilibrium point and the Lyapunov comparison functions. Compared with a previous result, the obtained theorem removes the monotonicity assumption on the squared distance function along system trajectories. An example on the two-dimensional hyperbolic space illustrates the result and shows that this monotonicity condition may fail. 

Keywords: uniform asymptotic stability; Lyapunov theorem; Riemannian manifold; equilibrium point; region of attraction.

This paper studies sufficient conditions for uniform asymptotic stability of equilibrium points of nonautonomous systems (1.1) evolving on general Riemannian manifolds, together with explicit estimates of their regions of attraction. The central issue is to examine the applicability of the classical Lyapunov theorem in this framework and, when applicable, to provide an explicit estimate of the region of attraction. Classical Lyapunov stability theory states that if a Lyapunov function associated with an equilibrium point can be constructed, then the equilibrium point is uniformly asymptotically stable; see [3, Theorem 4.9]. Moreover, the proof of the theorem usually yields an explicit estimate of the region of attraction, which is important for characterizing sets of admissible initial states. The significance of the Lyapunov theorem and its region-of-attraction estimate may be summarized as follows: 1. Theoretical foundation: without explicitly solving the differential equation of the system, the Lya-punov theorem provides a powerful and concise framework for stability analysis. Its basic idea is to construct a scalar function with an energy-like decay property, thereby replacing the task of solving the differential equation by the construction of a function and the verification of inequalities [4]. 2. Practical design and stability guarantees: the estimate of the region of attraction is not merely a by-product of a theoretical proof, but rather a quantitative tool of practical importance. This point is illustrated by Khalil’s transient-stability example for a power system in his classical textbook; see [3, Section 8.2, p.313]. In practical operation, it is not sufficient to know only that an equilibrium point is asymptotically stable; one must also determine its region of attraction in order to evaluate the capability of returning to the equilibrium after perturbations. Hence, estimating regions of attraction is crucial for controller design, robustness analysis, and stability guarantees in areas such as robotics and aerospace engineering. This paper investigates sufficient conditions for uniform asymptotic stability of equilibrium points of systems on Riemannian manifolds and explicit estimates of their regions of attraction, mainly for the following reasons: 21. Many mechanical systems are naturally described by ordinary differential equations on Riemannian manifolds; see, for instance, [5–7]. In such systems, the Riemannian metric is usually determined by the kinetic energy of the system; see [6, Lemmas 4.29 & 4.30, p.169]. For example, the con-figuration space of a two-link robotic arm is the Lie group (SO (3) × R3)2, and the kinetic energy naturally endows this manifold with a Riemannian metric; see [6, Example 4.4, p.147 & Exam-ple 4.32, p.170]. In the absence of external torques, the inertial motion of a rigid body corresponds to the geodesic equation of this metric, namely an autonomous ordinary differential equation on the manifold. When time-varying external forces are present, the equations of motion become nonau-tonomous ordinary differential equations on the manifold; see [6, Proposition 4.59, p.195]. Stability and stabilization problems for systems evolving on manifolds have also been studied from the viewpoint of control theory; see, for instance, [8, 9]. These works are concerned with feed-back stabilization of control systems on manifolds, and are complementary to the present paper, whose purpose is to establish Lyapunov-type sufficient conditions and explicit estimates of regions of attraction for given nonautonomous systems. 2. Classical Lyapunov stability theory and its estimates of regions of attraction for equilibrium points are mostly developed in Euclidean spaces. When the state space of the system is generalized to a Riemannian manifold, a natural and important question is whether the classical results can be directly extended and how the region of attraction should be estimated. At present, studies of Lyapunov theory for systems on Riemannian manifolds mainly focus on autonomous systems, for example [10, Corollary 20], [6, Section 6.1], and [11, Proposition 4.1 & Corollary 4.5]. However, these works either do not provide estimates of regions of attraction or directly assert global uniform asymptotic stability of the equilibrium point. 3. Existing literature, such as [12, Theorem 1] and [11, Corollary 5.9.13, p.251], shows that many systems defined on manifolds do not admit globally uniformly asymptotically stable equilibrium points. Therefore, from a practical viewpoint, it is necessary to estimate regions of attraction for equilibrium points that are only locally uniformly asymptotically stable. 4. Existing literature on Lyapunov theorems for systems on Riemannian manifolds remains relatively limited. Although [13] establishes a theory of uniform asymptotic stability for equilibrium points and provides corresponding estimates of regions of attraction, Theorem 2.1(iii) of [13] requires the additional monotonicity condition 

dxρ2(x, x ∗)( f (t, x )) ≤ 0. (1.6) However, this condition may be difficult to verify and may even fail for some systems; see Exam-ple 4.1 below. A main purpose of the present paper is to remove this monotonicity condition. In summary, establishing a theory of uniform asymptotic stability for equilibrium points of systems on Riemannian manifolds, together with explicit estimates of regions of attraction, is of important theoretical and practical significance. To establish a Lyapunov theorem for system (1.1) on Riemannian manifolds, we first present the corre-sponding stability result in the Euclidean setting. More precisely, Theorem 3.1 gives sufficient conditions for uniform asymptotic stability of an equilibrium point of system (1.1) in the case M = Rn; at the same time, based on the class-K functions α1(·) and α2(·) introduced in (1.4), it gives an explicit estimate of the region of attraction. On this basis, by using a mapping depending on the Riemannian metric, we transform system (1.1) on the manifold M into an auxiliary system on the tangent space Tx∗ M at the equilibrium point x∗. Since this auxiliary system is defined on the flat Euclidean space Tx∗ M , Theorem 3.1 can be applied directly, and thus one obtains a Lyapunov theorem for system (1.1) on a general Riemannian manifold, namely Theorem 3.2. 3Furthermore, to illustrate the effectiveness of Theorem 3.2, we provide an example. The novelty of this paper can be summarized as follows: 1. In the case where the state space is Euclidean, we establish Lyapunov-type sufficient conditions guaranteeing uniform asymptotic stability and give an explicit estimate of the region of attraction. Compared with existing Euclidean results (see [3, Theorem 4.9, p.152 & Theorem 4.10, p.154]), our estimate is sharper; see Remark 3.1(2). 2. In Theorem 3.2, the obtained estimate of the region of attraction of a uniformly asymptotically sta-ble equilibrium point depends on the injectivity radius at this equilibrium point. This geometric constraint has a substantial influence on the estimate of the region of attraction and makes effective estimates of the injectivity radius at the equilibrium point a key step. When the manifold M is complete and simply connected, the injectivity radius can be bounded in terms of sectional curva-ture, as explained in Remark 3.2. In Example 4.1, we apply the Hadamard theorem to estimate the injectivity radius at the equilibrium point. 3. Compared with Theorem 2.1(iii) of [13], Theorem 3.2 removes the additional monotonicity assump-tion (1.6) on the squared distance function along system trajectories. In [13], this condition leads to the region-of-attraction estimate Bx∗ (r0). In the present paper, without this monotonicity condition, we obtain the estimate Bx∗ (rv ). In general, rv may be smaller than r0, but if the comparison func-tions α1 and α2 have the same limiting value at r0, then rv = r0 and the same whole ball Bx∗ (r0)

is obtained. The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the necessary preliminaries. Sec-tion 3 states the main results, Theorems 3.1 and 3.2. Section 4 gives an example. Section 5 gives the proofs of Theorems 3.1 and 3.2.

Remark 3.2. Theorem 3.2 extends Theorem 3.1 from the Euclidean setting to the Riemannian-manifold setting. We further compare Theorem 3.2 with the preceding Lyapunov theorem in [13]. 1. Unlike the Euclidean case, the radius r0 in Theorem 3.2 is constrained not only by the domain D of the system, but also by the injectivity radius at the equilibrium point. The injectivity radius is closely related to the sectional curvature of the Riemannian manifold. Several estimates showing how the injectivity radius of a complete simply connected Riemannian manifold is controlled by sectional curvature are given in [2, Corollary 3.3, p.202], [17, Theorem 1.6], [15, Lemma 6.4.7, p.258], and [16, Proposition 12.9, p.352]. Therefore, the Lyapunov theorem on a Riemannian manifold is intrinsically constrained by the differential-geometric structure of the manifold at the equilibrium point. In particular, when M = Rn is equipped with the Euclidean metric, the sectional curvature is zero and the injectivity radius is infinite. In this case, the definition of r0 in Theorem 3.2 agrees with that in Theorem 3.1, and the conclusion naturally reduces to the Euclidean case. 2. We next compare Theorem 3.2 with Theorem 2.1(iii) of [13]. In Theorem 2.1(iii) of [13], besides the Lyapunov-type assumptions there, one needs the additional monotonicity condition
